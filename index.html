How I work

My current work sits across four connected responsibilities: bringing evidence together, assessing what it means, building practical systems, and contributing that expertise through teaching and scientific cooperation.

  1. Synthesise

    Bring together occurrence data, food-consumption information, toxicological reference values and scientific literature while keeping their provenance and limitations visible.

  2. Assess

    Evaluate exposure, uncertainty, risk–benefit trade-offs and priorities, translating heterogeneous evidence into clear and proportionate conclusions.

  3. Build

    Turn fragmented evidence into databases, toolkits and transparent methods for exposure assessment, risk comparison and monitoring priorities.

  4. Share responsibility

    Teach and supervise, review scientific work, and contribute through European networks, expert groups and public scientific governance.

Current research

Which chemical risks should food monitoring and assessment focus on when data and resources are limited?

Postdoctoral research
2025–2028 · BIOR

From Data to Decisions

I lead a postdoctoral project building a harmonised database of chemical-contaminant occurrence data for Latvian food and feed, linking monitoring and research data with food consumption and toxicological reference values. The aim is to compare chemical risks on a consistent basis, make uncertainty visible and identify priorities for further assessment and monitoring.

The contaminants list and database specification are complete. I am now working on linking the data and developing transparent risk-prioritisation methods. Literature-data extraction is another strand of this work.

Selected research

Exposure and guidance

Fish consumption: weighing risks and benefits

As first author of a 2026 pilot study, I assessed contaminant exposure alongside the nutritional benefits of Latvian inland fish to derive weekly-consumption guidance. The work connects occurrence and food-composition data with a practical dietary question.

Read the pilot risk-benefit assessment · Foods, 2026

Evidence synthesis

Bringing bee-product contaminant evidence together

Within BeSafeBeeHoney, I coordinate literature search, screening and data extraction on contaminants in honey, pollen, bee bread, royal jelly and propolis. This work brings scattered occurrence evidence together to examine dietary exposure and gaps in monitoring.

BeSafeBeeHoney · COST CA22105 Related work: glyphosate occurrence · Food Control, 2026 · Co-author

Analytical methods

Finding chemicals beyond routine target lists

My first-author study of paper straws used high-resolution mass spectrometry and non-target screening to investigate potentially hazardous chemicals in food-contact materials. This earlier analytical background informs how I interpret contaminant evidence and its limitations in risk assessment.

Read the paper-straw screening study · Food Additives & Contaminants: Part A, 2020
