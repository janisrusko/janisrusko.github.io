CHEMICAL RISK ASSESSMENT / EVIDENCE SYSTEMS / DECISION SUPPORT

Janis Rusko

I work on evidence systems for chemical risk assessment in food safety: structuring occurrence and exposure data, comparing risks, and translating results into prioritisation, guidance, and decision-support methods. Analytical chemistry and non-target analysis are the technical foundation.

01

CURRENT EMPHASIS · 2026

Current Work

Current work is organised around three connected problems: building usable occurrence data, producing interpretable dietary assessments, and improving risk prioritisation.

  1. Occurrence-data infrastructure

    IN DEVELOPMENT

    Developing the Occurrence Router, a workflow for extracting and structuring occurrence data from scientific literature and other evidence sources for downstream risk-assessment use.

  2. Dietary exposure and risk-benefit assessment

    Conducting applied exposure, chemical-risk, and risk-benefit assessments for food contaminants, with outputs designed for guidance and policy-facing decisions.

  3. Risk prioritisation and decision-support methods

    Developing methods and evidence structures for ranking substances, identifying gaps, and directing assessment and monitoring effort.

02

Research Direction

The longer-term programme is to improve the interface between chemical evidence and decisions: from fragmented data to traceable prioritisation, interpretable assessments, and usable guidance.

  1. Risk prioritisation

    How should limited assessment and monitoring resources be directed toward the most relevant chemical risks?

  2. Evidence systems

    How can fragmented occurrence, exposure, and hazard evidence be structured into usable assessment workflows?

  3. Assessment and guidance

    How can exposure data be translated into robust, interpretable conclusions and proportionate guidance?

  4. Automation and data methods

    Where can automation, AI-assisted extraction, and modern data methods improve the quality and efficiency of risk assessment?
